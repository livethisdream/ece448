\textbf{Instructions:} Submit this packet as one complete submission by the date indicated on
Gradescope. This is an individual assignment, but you may collaborate, with appropriate
documentation. Bring your PlutoSDR and its two antennas, a short SMA cable, and the 30 dB SMA
attenuator. Every step transmits from your PlutoSDR at 915 MHz: through the cable and the
attenuator from its TX port to its RX port, or over the air at low power when I say so.
Connect the cable and attenuator, or the antennas, before you run the flowgraph.

\textbf{Part 1: Predict} the two shaded cells in Table B, and write your own line of text in
Table A, before the lab period, attaching your reasoning.

\textbf{Part 2: Measure} the indicated quantities in class, and paste Figures 1 to 4.

\textbf{Part 3: Analyze} the results according to the prompts in this section, after the lab.

\textbf{Grading:} I grade the packet against Objective 4.4 on the course's 1 / 0 scale, and you
may revise it under course policy. I grade each Part 3 answer on its claim, the evidence from
your own table and figures, the reasoning that connects them, and the uncertainty in your
readings.

\begin{procbox}
\procstep{1--4}{\textbf{Send your line} with \texttt{L10-pam-pluto.grc} and compare the
constellation before and after carrier recovery with your prediction.}
\procstep{5--6}{\textbf{Which way up?} Restart the link four times and find the Polarity
setting that delivers your line on each run.}
\procstep{7--9}{\textbf{A second radio's oscillator}: switch on a TX oscillator error the

\procstep{10--13}{\textbf{Too little, too much}: drop the signal toward the noise floor, then
overload the receiver, and watch the samples, the constellations, and the line.}
\end{procbox}
