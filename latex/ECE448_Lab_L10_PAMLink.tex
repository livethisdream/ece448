\fullwidth{\textbf{Learning Objective 4.4: } I can implement a PAM link that transmits and
receives ASCII text end-to-end.}

\vspace{2pt}

This packet is the L\labLesson\ Lab, in three parts, and you turn it in once, complete,
before Lesson 11. Do \textbf{Part 1} on your own before the lab period: run the link
flowgraph and make the predictions you will check at the bench. \textbf{Part 2} is
your own measurements, recorded in class: Questions 3 to 5 on your own laptop, and
Question 6 at a PlutoSDR station with your team. Do \textbf{Part 3} on your own after
the lab: compare the measurements with the predictions and answer the analysis
questions.

\textbf{Grading.} One submission, graded against Objective 4.4 on the course's
1 / 0 scale, with revision allowed under course policy. Paste screenshots where a
question asks for one.

\textbf{Transmitting.} In class we transmit only into a cable, into a shielded
enclosure, or at very low power in an ISM band, and only when the lesson says to.
Questions 1 to 5 run on your laptop and transmit nothing. Question 6 transmits from a
PlutoSDR's TX port into a cable to the same Pluto's RX port, at 915 MHz. Connect the
cable before you run the flowgraph, and never connect an antenna in this lab.

The flowgraphs are on the L10 lesson page, \texttt{L10-pam-tx.grc} and
\texttt{L10-pam-link.grc}, and on the L10 Lab page, \texttt{L10-pam-pluto.grc}. Every
number below assumes their settings: \texttt{samp\_rate} = 32 kHz, \texttt{sps} = 8,
so 4 kbaud, $\alpha = 0.35$, and the default line \texttt{USAFA ECE 448: ASCII over
PAM.}, 30 characters and a newline, 31 bytes.

\question \textbf{Predictions.} You will check each of these at the bench.

\begin{parts}

	\begin{minipage}[t]{0.58\linewidth}
		\part Each line is a packet: a 64-bit access code, a 32-bit header, and the 31
		bytes of the line. Find the bits per packet, the time per packet, and the number
		of packets in 30 s.
		\begin{solution}[1.0in]
		\eq{ 64 + 32 + 8 \times 31 &= 344\ \text{bits} \\
			344 / 4000 &= 86\ \text{ms} \\
			30 / 0.086 &\approx 349 }
		\end{solution}
	\end{minipage}\hfill%
	\begin{minipage}[t]{0.37\linewidth}
		\raggedleft \vspace{12pt}
		bits = \ansbox[1.0in]{344}

		\vspace{4pt}
		time = \ansbox[1.0in]{86 ms}

		\vspace{4pt}
		in 30 s = \ansbox[1.0in]{349}
	\end{minipage}

	\part Choose a line of text of your own, such as your class year and name. Write
	it below, count its bytes with the newline, and write the 16-bit length field the
	header will carry and the 8 bits of its first character.
		\begin{solution}[0.8in]
		The answer depends on the line. For \texttt{C1C Jane Doe}, 12 characters and a newline make
		13 bytes, so the length field is \texttt{0000000000001101}, and `C' is 0x43,
		\texttt{01000011}.
		\end{solution}

	\part Predict the SNR at the slicer, $8/\sigma^2$, the bit error rate,
	$Q(\sqrt{\text{SNR}})$, and the fraction of lines that arrive intact,
	$(1 - \text{BER})^{280}$, for each noise amplitude.
	In Python, $Q(x)$ is \texttt{0.5*math.erfc(x/math.sqrt(2))}.

	\begin{center}
	\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.25}
	\begin{tabular}{| l | K{0.62in} | K{0.62in} | K{0.62in} | K{0.62in} | K{0.62in} | K{0.62in} |}
	\hline
	$\sigma$ & $0.5$ & $0.7$ & $0.8$ & $0.9$ & $1.0$ & $1.2$ \\\hline
	SNR (dB) & \kv{$15.1$} & \kv{$12.1$} & \kv{$11.0$} & \kv{$9.9$} & \kv{$9.0$} & \kv{$7.4$} \\\hline
	BER & \kv{$8\times10^{-9}$} & \kv{$2.7\times10^{-5}$} & \kv{$2.0\times10^{-4}$} & \kv{$8.4\times10^{-4}$} & \kv{$2.3\times10^{-3}$} & \kv{$9.2\times10^{-3}$} \\\hline

	\begin{minipage}[t]{0.58\linewidth}
		\part At a clock error of 10,000 ppm, how many samples does each symbol span at
		the receiver? Without timing recovery, after how many symbols, and how many
		milliseconds, would a fixed sampling point slip one whole symbol?

		\eq{ 8/1.01 &= 7.92\ \text{samples} \\

		slip in \ansbox[1.0in]{25 ms}

	\hline

		\begin{solution}[0.8in]
		Symbol Sync holds every line to 5000 ppm and most of them to 20,000 ppm. The
		30,000 ppm result varies from run to run. By 40,000 ppm the loop can no longer

		samples per symbol, so it drifts and smears even where every line arrives.

	while the Symbol Sync output stays at $\pm 1$.
		\begin{solution}[1.2in]
		Each symbol spans 7.92 samples, but the eye sink cuts the waveform every 8, so
		each trace starts a little later in its symbol and the eye slides across the
		display. Symbol Sync follows the real symbol timing, so its samples stay at the
		center of each symbol.
		\end{solution}

\fullwidth{\large\textbf{Part 3: Writeup} \normalsize(individual, after the lab)}

\question \textbf{Analysis.}

\begin{parts}

	\part Compare your noise table with Question 2(c). Find the SNR at which your link
	first lost lines and the SNR at which almost none arrived, and say what SNR a
	Lesson 2 link budget for this link should deliver at the slicer.
		\begin{solution}[1.5in]
		Lines start to fail near 12 dB ($\sigma = 0.7$), the link loses half of them at 9 dB, and
		almost none arrive at 7.4 dB, so the change takes about 5 dB. Each line needs
		all 280 header and payload bits right, so a modest rise in the bit error rate
		loses many lines. A budget should deliver at least 12 dB at the slicer, for
		99\% of lines, plus a fade margin. Measured lines run a few percent below the
		prediction because Symbol Sync's sampling instant jitters.
		\end{solution}

	\part Your link held every line to at least 5000 ppm. Compare that with two radios
	whose crystals are accurate to 20 ppm each. Then use Question 2(d) to say what would
	happen to the link if you replaced Symbol Sync with Keep 1 in N.
		\begin{solution}[1.5in]
		Two 20 ppm crystals can differ by 40 ppm, more than 100 times smaller than 5000
		ppm, so clock error does not limit this link. With Keep 1 in N, a 40 ppm error
		slips the sampling point one whole symbol every $1/(40\times10^{-6}) = 25{,}000$
		symbols, 6.25 s, and it crosses the zero crossings, where about half the
		decisions are wrong, every few seconds. Lines would fail in bursts, and at
		10,000 ppm the sampling point would slip a symbol every 25 ms, so almost no line
		would arrive.
		\end{solution}

	\part Explain why the working polarity changed between runs on the Pluto, and
	propose a change to the receiver that would make the polarity switch unnecessary.
		\begin{solution}[1.5in]
		The Costas Loop locks to the carrier's phase, but a 2-PAM signal looks the same
		rotated by 180$^\circ$, so the loop can lock either way, and it picks one at
		random each time it starts. Locked upside down, every bit inverts, and the
		access code never matches. Either of two fixes works: search for both the access code and
		its inverse, and flip the bits when the inverse matches; or send the data
		differentially, as a change or no change from one bit to the next, which an
		inversion does not affect.
		\end{solution}

\end{parts}
