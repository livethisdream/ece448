demodulator (BPSK, QPSK, 8PSK), recover carrier and symbol timing, and verify
constellation behavior against theory.}

\vspace{2pt}

This packet is the L\labLesson\ Lab, in three parts, and you turn it in once, complete,
before Lesson 16. Do \textbf{Part 1} on your own before the lab period: run the link
flowgraph and make the predictions you will check at the bench. \textbf{Part 2} is
your own measurements, recorded in class: Questions 3 to 6 on your own laptop, and
Question 7 at a PlutoSDR station with your team. Do \textbf{Part 3} on your own after
the lab: compare the measurements with the predictions and answer the analysis
questions.

\textbf{Grading.} You submit the packet once, we grade it against Objective 6.4 on
the course's 1 / 0 scale, and you may revise it under course policy. Paste screenshots
where a question asks for one.

\textbf{Transmitting.} In class we transmit only into a cable, into a shielded
enclosure, or at very low power in an ISM band, and only when the lesson says to.
Questions 1 to 6 run on your laptop and transmit nothing. Question 7 transmits from a
PlutoSDR's TX port, through a cable with an inline 30 dB SMA attenuator, to the same
Pluto's RX port, at 915 MHz. Connect the cable and attenuator before you run the
flowgraph, and never connect an antenna in this lab. Never set TX attenuation below
30 dB or change the slider's range; the RX input is rated to only a few dBm. We have
run the PlutoSDR flowgraph only in simulation, so Question 7 is its first run on
hardware.

The flowgraphs are on the L15 lesson page, \texttt{L15-psk-tx.grc} and
\texttt{L15-psk-link.grc}, and on the L15 Lab page, \texttt{L15-psk-pluto.grc}. Every
number below assumes their settings: 4,000 symbols per second, RRC pulses with
$\alpha = 0.35$ at 8 samples per symbol, Gray-coded points, a Costas Loop bandwidth of
0.02, and the default message. Lesson 3 gives the bit error rate with ideal
synchronization:
\[ P_b^\text{QPSK} = Q\left(\sqrt{\frac{2E_b}{N_0}}\right), \qquad
   P_b^\text{8PSK} \approx \frac{2}{3}\, Q\left(\sqrt{\frac{6E_b}{N_0}}\,\sin\frac{\pi}{8}\right). \]

\begin{parts}

		\end{solution}

	\part Predict the bit error rate from Lesson 3 at each setting you will measure in

	\begin{center}

		channel phase within $180^\circ/M$ of $0^\circ$: within $45^\circ$ for QPSK, at

		\end{solution}

		\begin{solution}[1.3in]
		The detector's range is $\pm 180^\circ/M$: $90^\circ$, $45^\circ$, and

\question \textbf{Build a carrier-recovery loop.} Start GRC with an empty canvas. Do not
open the lesson's flowgraphs: build the 8PSK carrier-recovery flowgraph yourself as the
lab page lists, with a 20 Hz frequency offset.

	must be black, not red.
		\begin{solution}[1.5in]
		The flowgraph matches the lab page's list: Random Source, Chunks to Symbols with
		the 8PSK table, Throttle, a Multiply with a complex Signal Source at \texttt{foff},
		an Add with a Noise Source, and a Costas Loop of order 8, with a two-input QT GUI
		Constellation Sink and the loop's frequency output, scaled by
		$4000/2\pi$, on a QT GUI Number Sink.
		\end{solution}

	\begin{minipage}[t]{0.58\linewidth}
		\part Paste a screenshot of the running window, and record the loop's frequency
		reading at offsets of 20 Hz and $-60$ Hz.
		\begin{solution}[1.2in]
		The input is a ring and the output is eight clusters at $22.5^\circ$ plus
		multiples of $45^\circ$. The reading settles within about 1 Hz of the offset: near
		20 Hz, then near $-60$ Hz.
		\end{solution}
	\end{minipage}\hfill%
	\begin{minipage}[t]{0.37\linewidth}
		\raggedleft \vspace{12pt}
		20 Hz: \ansbox[1.0in]{$\approx 20$ Hz}

		\vspace{4pt}
		$-60$ Hz: \ansbox[1.0in]{$\approx -60$ Hz}
	\end{minipage}

	\part Change the Costas Loop's order from 8 to 2 and run it again. Describe the
	output and the frequency reading, and explain why the loop needs the right order.
		\begin{solution}[1.2in]
		The output stays a ring and the reading wanders near 0 Hz instead of settling at
		20 Hz. The order-2 detector, $I \times Q$, expects two points on the I axis.
		Averaged over the eight 8PSK points its error is zero at every phase, so it gives
		the loop nothing to lock to. The detector has to know the constellation to measure
		the error to the nearest point.
		\end{solution}

\end{parts}

\fullwidth{\large\textbf{Part 3: Writeup} \normalsize(individual, after the lab)}

\question \textbf{Analysis.}

\begin{parts}

	\part Compare your BER table with Question 2(b). Estimate how many dB of $E_b/N_0$
	the link loses to its loops and how many to differential coding, and state the
	$E_b/N_0$ a Lesson 2 link budget should deliver for QPSK at a BER of $10^{-4}$.
		\begin{solution}[1.5in]
		The measured points sit 1.2 to 2.5 times above the differential prediction and
		about twice the theory with coding off. On a curve that falls a decade every 1 to
		2 dB, a factor of 2 is about 0.4 dB, so differential coding adds about 0.4 dB and
		the loops about 0.2 to 0.3 dB. The lesson measured 8.9 dB for differential QPSK at
		$10^{-4}$; a budget should deliver that plus an allowance for the real radio,
		about 1.4 dB in Lesson 3, plus a fade margin.
		\end{solution}

	\part Compare the 8PSK pull-in range with your estimate in Question 2(d), explain
	why it is smaller than QPSK's, and say what a link between two different Plutos,
	each accurate to 25 ppm at 915 MHz, would need before its Costas Loop.
		\begin{solution}[1.5in]
		The 8PSK loop pulled in from about 175 Hz against about 375 Hz for QPSK. Its
		detector measures an error only up to $22.5^\circ$, so a spinning constellation
		leaves that range sooner. Two Plutos can be up to $2 \times 25 \times 10^{-6}
		\times 915\ \text{MHz} \approx 46$ kHz apart, hundreds of times the range, so the
		receiver needs a coarse frequency correction first, such as an FLL Band-Edge
		block, or a higher symbol rate with the loop scaled to it.
		\end{solution}

	\part Compare Question 6 with Question 2(c) and Question 7(b). Explain why the
	working settings were where they were, why the Pluto runs behaved as they did, and
	why a real mPSK link uses differential coding even though it raises the BER.
		\begin{solution}[1.5in]
		The loop locks to the rotation nearest the received phase, so with coding off the
		text arrives only when the phase is within $180^\circ/M$ of $0^\circ$: 5 of 18
		settings for QPSK and 3 for 8PSK. On the Pluto the phase is random at every
		start, so about one run in four locks the right way. Differential coding makes
		every rotation work and limits a cycle slip to one symbol, which matters more than the
		roughly 0.4 dB it adds.
		\end{solution}

\end{parts}
