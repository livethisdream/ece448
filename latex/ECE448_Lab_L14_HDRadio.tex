\fullwidth{\textbf{Learning Objective 6.3: } I can describe how HD Radio works and
demodulate an HD Radio signal.}

\vspace{2pt}

This packet is the L\labLesson\ Lab, in three parts, and you turn it in once, complete,
before Lesson 15. Do \textbf{Part 1} on your own before the lab period: run the sideband
finder and make the predictions you will use at the bench. \textbf{Part 2} is your own
measurements, recorded in class on your own laptop with your RTL-SDR. Do \textbf{Part 3}
on your own after the lab: compare the measurements with the rules and answer the
analysis questions.

\textbf{Grading.} One submission, graded against Objective 6.3 on the course's
1 / 0 scale, with revision allowed under course policy. Paste screenshots where a
question asks for one.

\textbf{Transmitting.} Nothing in this lab transmits. The RTL-SDR receives only, and both
captures came from GNU Radio flowgraphs, not from an antenna.

The flowgraphs are on the L14 lesson page, \texttt{L14-hd-test-tx.grc} and
\texttt{L14-hd-find.grc}, and the capture \texttt{L14-lab-capture.cu8} is on the L14 Lab
page. Both captures are simulated: unsigned 8-bit I and Q at 1,488,375 samples per second,
the format the RTL-SDR and nrsc5 record. Every number below uses the hybrid values from
the lesson: subcarrier spacing $\Delta f = 1{,}488{,}375/4096 = 363.37$ Hz, sidebands from
subcarrier 356 to 546 on each side, partitions of 19 subcarriers, and an extended hybrid
station adding one, two, or four partitions inside subcarrier 356 on each side.

\question \textbf{Predictions.} You will use each of these at the bench.

\begin{parts}

	\part A station adds one, two, or four partitions inside subcarrier 356 on each side.
	For each, find the new inner subcarrier number, its frequency from the channel center,
	and the number of subcarriers in one sideband.

	\begin{center}
	\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.25}
	\begin{tabular}{| l | K{1.0in} | K{1.0in} | K{1.0in} |}
	\hline
	Added partitions & 1 & 2 & 4 \\\hline
	Inner subcarrier & \kv{337} & \kv{318} & \kv{280} \\\hline
	Inner edge (kHz) & \kv{122.5} & \kv{115.6} & \kv{101.7} \\\hline
	Subcarriers per side & \kv{210} & \kv{229} & \kv{267} \\\hline
	\end{tabular}
	\end{center}

		\begin{solution}[0.5in]
		Each partition is 19 subcarriers, so the inner edge moves to $356 - 19n$, and the
		sideband gains $19n$ subcarriers over its 191. With four partitions the digital
		signal starts 101.7 kHz from center.
		\end{solution}

	\part Find the frequency sink's bin width, the number of bins one 69,041 Hz sideband
	spans, and the number of subcarriers per bin, for an FFT of 1024 points and of 8192
	points at 1,488,375 samples per second.

	\begin{center}
	\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.25}
	\begin{tabular}{| l | K{1.1in} | K{1.1in} | K{1.3in} |}
	\hline
	FFT size & Bin width (Hz) & Bins per sideband & Subcarriers per bin \\\hline
	1024 & \kv{1453.5} & \kv{47.5} & \kv{4} \\\hline
	8192 & \kv{181.7} & \kv{380} & \kv{0.5} \\\hline
	\end{tabular}
	\end{center}

		\begin{solution}[0.4in]
		$1{,}488{,}375/N$; with 8192 points each subcarrier spans two bins, so an edge can be
		read to within about one subcarrier.
		\end{solution}

	\begin{minipage}[t]{0.58\linewidth}
		\part A hybrid station runs its digital signal at the FCC's $-14$ dBc limit, with equal
		sidebands. Find each sideband's level and each subcarrier's level in dBc. If its analog
		signal is 6 kW, how many watts is the digital signal in total?
		\begin{solution}[1.2in]
		\eq{ -14 - 10\log_{10} 2 &= -17.0\ \text{dBc} \\
			-14 - 10\log_{10} 382 &= -39.8\ \text{dBc} \\
			6000 \times 10^{-1.4} &= 239\ \text{W} }
		\end{solution}
	\end{minipage}\hfill%
	\begin{minipage}[t]{0.37\linewidth}
		\raggedleft \vspace{12pt}
		sideband \ansbox[1.1in]{$-17.0$ dBc}

		\vspace{4pt}
		subcarrier \ansbox[1.1in]{$-39.8$ dBc}

		\vspace{4pt}
		total \ansbox[1.1in]{239 W}
	\end{minipage}

	\begin{minipage}[t]{0.58\linewidth}
		\part An extended hybrid sideband with two added partitions has the same power in every
		subcarrier. A meter set to subcarriers 356 to 546 measures only part of it. By how many
		dB does that meter read low?
		\begin{solution}[0.8in]
		The meter sees 191 of the 229 subcarriers:
		\eq{ 10\log_{10}(191/229) = -0.79\ \text{dB} }
		\end{solution}
	\end{minipage}\hfill%
	\begin{minipage}[t]{0.37\linewidth}
		\raggedleft \vspace{12pt}
		\ansbox[1.1in]{0.79 dB low}
	\end{minipage}

\end{parts}

\fullwidth{\large\textbf{Part 3: Writeup} \normalsize(individual, after the lab)}

\question \textbf{Analysis.}

\begin{parts}

	\part Compare the live station's sideband levels with the $-20$ dBc and $-14$ dBc
	rules and the 10 dB limit on asymmetry. How is the station configured?
		\begin{solution}[1.5in]
		Answers vary. A total near $-20$ dBc means the original level; near $-14$ dBc, the
		increase the FCC allowed in 2010. Equal sidebands within about 1 dB are symmetric; a
		larger difference, up to 10 dB, means the station lowered one side, usually to
		protect a neighbor on that side.
		\end{solution}

	\part Explain how the lab capture's edges and meter readings identified its
	configuration, and give one reason a station would choose each feature you found.
		\begin{solution}[1.5in]
		The inner edges at subcarriers $\pm 318$ are two partitions inside 356, which is
		extended hybrid; a station adds partitions for more data capacity, which leaves less room
		for the analog signal. The upper sideband is 4 dB above the lower, which is asymmetric;
		a station lowers one sideband to protect a neighbor on that side. The total of
		$-15.5$ dBc is above the original $-20$ dBc and within the FCC's $-14$ dBc limit.
		\end{solution}

	\part nrsc5 cannot decode \texttt{L14-hd-test.cu8}, although its sidebands have the right
	subcarriers, spacing, and guard. Name two things nrsc5 needs from a signal that the test
	file does not provide.
		\begin{solution}[1.5in]
		Any two: reference subcarriers that carry NRSC-5's known synchronization and control
		bits, which nrsc5 uses to find the timing, frequency, and phase; data subcarriers
		that carry real NRSC-5 frames, scrambled, coded, and interleaved as the standard
		specifies, rather than random QPSK; compressed audio and station data inside those
		frames; and the standard's own symbol shaping rather than the test file's cyclic
		prefix with a short rolloff.
		\end{solution}

\end{parts}
